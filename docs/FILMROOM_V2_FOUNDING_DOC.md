# FILM ROOM V2
## A venture-backable version of the company

Prepared for: Shaan Kalgaonkar  
Date: April 2, 2026  
Status: Strategy reset

---

## 1. The New Thesis

Film Room should not start as "Duolingo for all football fans."

That framing is too broad, too soft, and too easy to admire without using. Venture-backable companies usually begin with a narrow user who has a real recurring need, not a giant audience with vague interest.

The right version of Film Room is:

**The daily football recognition training app for serious high school and college skill-position players.**

Start with one wedge:

- High school and college **quarterbacks**
- Then expand to **WRs**
- Then expand to **RBs**

This is not primarily an entertainment app. It is a **performance-improvement product**.

That matters because:

- the user has a real reason to train repeatedly
- the user can feel improvement
- the user has identity-based motivation
- the user is far more likely to pay for something that helps them perform
- coaches and teams become a credible second market later

The consumer-fan market can still exist later, but it should not be the wedge.

---

## 2. The Core Problem

Serious football players do physical reps and film study, but they do not have a mobile tool for high-frequency mental reps.

Today, a quarterback improves recognition through:

- team film sessions
- coach walkthroughs
- whiteboard/chalk talk
- static PDFs and playbook installs
- YouTube clips and scattered content

These are fragmented, time-bound, and not personalized.

What is missing is simple:

**A player should be able to take 10 recognition reps on their phone in 5 minutes and get smarter at reading the game.**

That is the actual company.

---

## 3. Initial Customer

### Primary Wedge

High school quarterbacks ages 14-18 who:

- care about improving football IQ
- already do film study or want to
- are trying to win a starting job, play varsity, or get recruited
- have parents or coaches willing to pay for useful training tools

### Why this user first

- high pain
- clear use case
- repeat behavior is plausible
- product value is concrete
- word of mouth inside teams is real
- success stories are legible

### Secondary expansion

After strong retention with QBs:

1. WRs
2. RBs
3. Team / coach dashboard
4. Serious fan mode as a separate growth surface

Do not build for fans and coaches on day one.

---

## 4. Product Positioning

### What Film Room is

Film Room is a mobile training app that gives quarterbacks short, repeatable recognition reps:

- identify the coverage
- make the read
- choose the throw
- understand why

Each rep is fast, visual, and coached.

### What Film Room is not

- not a generic football trivia app
- not a broad social sports app
- not a coach platform at launch
- not "AI for AI's sake"
- not a nine-position simulation engine on day one

The positioning should be:

**Get better at reading defenses in 5 minutes a day.**

That is much stronger than "study the game."

## 5. MVP

### One-sentence MVP

An iPhone app for quarterbacks that delivers daily defensive-recognition reps with trusted explanations and tracks improvement over time.

### In V1 MVP

- QB only
- Cover 2 / Cover 3 / Cover 4 / Cover 1 / man-match recognition
- 10-20 high-quality scenario templates, curated by humans
- multiple-choice read questions
- explanation after every answer
- daily training set
- weekly progress score
- account + session history
- simple difficulty progression: Beginner, Intermediate

### Explicitly cut from MVP

- WR, RB, TE, CB, S, LB, OC, DC modes
- leaderboards
- badges
- friend challenges
- share cards
- global daily challenge
- AI Q&A
- custom concept generation
- four-tier skill ladder
- B2B team dashboard
- huge playbook library

If it does not directly improve the core training loop for quarterbacks, cut it.

## 6. Content Strategy

This is the most important strategic change.

The product cannot rely on fully AI-generated football logic at launch.

If the content is even slightly fake, advanced users will not trust it, coaches will reject it, and the company dies before product-market fit.

### V2 content approach

Use a **curated-first system**:

- build a structured football concept map
- create high-quality scenario templates manually
- use AI only to vary wording, add explanations, and generate controlled variants
- every scenario must stay inside known-valid football logic

### Practical content pipeline

1. Define a concept library
   - coverage shell
   - leverage
   - safety rotation
   - route concept
   - progression rule
   - pressure look

2. Build hand-authored scenario templates
   - base diagram
   - correct read
   - common wrong answers
   - teaching explanation

3. Let AI generate bounded variations
   - different down/distance
   - different wording
   - slight route presentation changes
   - age/skill-appropriate explanation tone

4. Add human review for anything net-new

This is slower than "generate everything with Claude," but it is vastly more investable because it increases trust.

## 7. User Experience

### Core loop

1. Open app
2. Start today's reps
3. See defensive look
4. Choose the read / throw
5. Get coaching explanation
6. Finish 5-10 reps
7. See what improved and what to review next

### Why users come back

Not because of streaks.

They come back because:

- they want to get faster at recognition
- they feel improvement
- they can use what they learn at practice or in games
- coaches or teammates may care about their progress

### Habit design

The right cadence is probably **3-5 times per week**, not necessarily daily for everyone.

The product should support:

- a short daily mode during season
- a deeper prep mode before games
- a review mode after games/practice

That is more realistic than pretending this is a universal daily consumer habit.

## 8. Retention System

Retention should come from utility and identity, not generic gamification.

### Strong retention drivers

- clear evidence of improvement
- position-specific identity
- coach or parent validation
- seasonal rhythm
- progressive difficulty

### Retention features that actually matter

- weekly QB scorecard
- weak-area resurfacing
- "beat your prior reaction speed/accuracy"
- prep packs by concept
- coach-approved training plans later

### Weak retention features to de-prioritize

- badges
- vanity leaderboards
- empty streak mechanics
- social shares nobody wants to post

## 9. Monetization

The original one-time `$4.99` unlock is not the right long-term venture model.

It may help with early conversion testing, but it is not a durable business if users consume ongoing content and inference.

### Recommended business model

#### Free

- limited weekly QB reps
- progress preview
- one starter training track

#### Pro subscription

`$12/month` or `$79/year`

Includes:

- full QB training library
- advanced difficulty tracks
- weekly prep packs
- detailed concept analytics
- faster review loops

#### Team / coach product later

Potential future pricing:

- team subscription
- coach dashboard
- assign drills
- roster progress visibility

That is where real expansion potential lives.

### Why subscription is better

- aligns with ongoing value
- supports content/inference costs
- creates venture-scale revenue potential
- makes retained power users economically meaningful

If users will not subscribe, that is a signal that the product is not important enough yet.

## 10. Market

The initial market is not "all NFL fans."

The initial market is:

- serious youth football athletes
- especially quarterbacks
- then adjacent position groups
- then coaches and teams

### Why this is still a big enough wedge

This market is smaller than mass fans, but much more valuable:

- stronger need
- higher retention potential
- better willingness to pay
- easier storytelling
- better enterprise adjacency

The venture case is not based on capturing all football fans. It is based on:

1. winning a narrow, painful use case
2. expanding to adjacent player roles
3. layering team/coaching workflows
4. later opening a broader fan surface if desired

That is a much more credible expansion path.

---

## 11. Why This Could Become Venture-Backable

Film Room becomes venture-backable if it proves three things:

### 1. Users trust it

Serious players must believe:

- the football is real
- the coaching is good
- the reps help

### 2. Users retain

Not "this is cool."

Actual repeated use over weeks, especially in season.

### 3. Users pay

The retained segment must be willing to subscribe.

Without those three, this is a niche app. With them, it can become a category-defining sports training product.

## 13. What Must Be True for This to Work

These are the core assumptions. They must be tested fast.

### Assumption 1

Quarterbacks will voluntarily do mental reps on mobile several times per week.

### Assumption 2

They will trust a product that is not literally team film.

### Assumption 3

Parents, players, or coaches will pay for perceived improvement.

### Assumption 4

A narrow QB wedge can expand into a larger football training platform.

If any of these are false, the company should pivot before overbuilding.

---

## 14. Metrics That Matter

Do not optimize for downloads.

At pre-seed, the only metrics that matter are:

- activation rate
- reps completed in first week
- week-2 retention
- 30-day retention
- sessions per retained user
- free-to-paid conversion
- qualitative trust score from serious users

### Pre-seed proof targets

The company becomes meaningfully more fundable if it can show:

- 100+ active target users in the wedge
- 35%+ week-2 retention
- 20%+ day-30 retention among the target segment
- strong qualitative love from players/coaches
- 5-10%+ conversion to paid among retained users

These are directional targets, not guarantees, but the company needs this kind of signal.

## 15. Fundraising Story

The old story was:

"There are 112M NFL fans and no one built Duolingo for football."

That is a weak investor story.

The new story should be:

**Film Room helps serious quarterbacks improve recognition through short, repeatable mobile reps.**

We are starting with the highest-frequency mental training need in football, proving retention and trust with a narrow wedge, then expanding into adjacent positions and team workflows.

That is a much stronger pre-seed narrative because it is focused, believable, and expandable.

## 16. Build Priorities

### Must build first

- scenario player for QB reps
- trusted curated content system
- explanation engine
- progress history
- subscription infrastructure

### Must not overbuild first

- social graph
- multi-position engine
- broad gamification
- complex adaptive system
- fan-facing entertainment layer

The first product should feel like a sharp training tool, not a sports social app.

---

## 17. Final Call

The original Film Room concept is interesting but too broad to be venture-backable.

This V2 version is narrower, but much stronger:

- one user
- one urgent problem
- one repeatable behavior
- one credible monetization path
- one real expansion story

If Film Room can earn trust and retention with serious quarterbacks, it has a shot.

If it cannot, no amount of features, AI language, or market sizing will save it.
