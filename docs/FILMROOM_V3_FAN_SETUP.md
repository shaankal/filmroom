# FILM ROOM V3
## Fan-first, game-feel strategy

Prepared for: Shaan Kalgaonkar  
Date: April 2, 2026  
Status: Fan-market repositioning

---

## 1. The New Thesis

Film Room should not be positioned as a football education app for everyone.

That sounds useful, but not urgent. Fans do not wake up wanting to "train football IQ." They do wake up wanting to:

- win debates
- feel smarter than their friends
- dominate fantasy group chats
- predict what is coming before the snap
- prove they know ball

That is the right version of Film Room for a fan-first market.

**Film Room is a competitive football prediction game that makes fans feel smarter while watching live football.**

This is not primarily an edtech product.

It is a **sports game layer** on top of football viewing.

The learning still matters, but it should feel like a byproduct of playing, not homework.

---

## 2. The Core User

### Primary wedge

Football-obsessed fans ages 16-34 who:

- watch NFL weekly
- use fantasy, betting, or football Twitter/Reddit content
- care about sounding smart and being right
- enjoy trivia, ranking, prediction, and competition
- already talk football in group chats

### The actual first customer

Not all fans.

The first real customer is:

**the high-engagement fan who wants to test and prove their football instincts**

That includes:

- fantasy players
- football Reddit / X users
- group chat alpha types
- ex-players who still identify as "smart football guys"

### Why this wedge is better than "all fans"

- stronger motivation
- better repeat behavior
- more likely to share
- more likely to pay for status, depth, and edge
- more likely to form social loops

---

## 3. Product Positioning

### What Film Room is

Film Room is a mobile football game where fans read the field, make the call, and score points for being right.

Core fantasy:

**See the look. Make the call. Beat your friends.**

### What Film Room is not

- not a pure learning app
- not a static trivia app
- not a coach tool at launch
- not an AI toy that generates random plays
- not a generic second-screen stats app

### Positioning line

**The football game for fans who think they know ball.**

That is much stronger than "study the game."

---

## 4. The Core Product

### Product principle

The app must feel like a game first and a lesson second.

That means every session should answer one question:

**Was I right, and am I smarter than the people I know?**

### Core loop

1. Open Film Room
2. Enter a challenge, daily mode, or live game mode
3. See a pre-snap or tactical scenario
4. Make the call
5. Score points, earn rank movement, and see why
6. Share, compare, or challenge again

### Why fans return

- to protect or improve rank
- to play daily challenges
- to beat friends
- to test themselves during real games
- to feel sharper every Sunday

The retention engine is not "education."

It is **competition + identity + live football relevance**.

---

## 5. The Wedge Use Case

The most investable fan use case is not generic training.

It is:

**A daily and game-day football prediction game for smart fans.**

That means the product should center on three modes:

### 1. Daily Challenge

One polished scenario each day.

Purpose:

- easy habit loop
- same shared prompt for all users
- leaderboard and friend comparison anchor

### 2. Head-to-Head Challenges

Send a scenario set to a friend or group.

Purpose:

- natural social loop
- status and bragging rights
- repeatable asynchronous multiplayer

### 3. Sunday Live Mode

Timed prediction or recognition prompts tied to real NFL viewing behavior.

Purpose:

- attaches the product to an existing ritual
- gives fans a reason to open the app while games are on
- creates the strongest retention surface

If Film Room does not own game-day behavior, it will likely become an occasional novelty.

---

## 6. MVP

### One-sentence MVP

An iPhone app that lets football fans play quick tactical prediction challenges, compete with friends, and build a public football IQ score.

### In V1 MVP

- fan-only positioning
- one scenario format: pre-snap read / likely coverage / best throw
- curated scenario pack, not infinite generation
- Daily Challenge
- friend challenges via link or iMessage
- global score + percentile
- friends leaderboard
- short explanation after answer
- profile with streak, score, win rate
- Sunday mode as a lightweight live companion

### Explicitly cut from MVP

- all player-position simulation depth
- full playbook library
- coach mode
- B2B team features
- too many question types
- custom AI scenario prompts
- heavy adaptive-learning infrastructure
- broad content encyclopedia

The MVP should feel like:

**Wordle meets football IQ meets fantasy-group-chat competition.**

That is the right mental model.

## 7. Content Strategy

This is still the most important quality issue.

The content cannot feel fake, random, or like AI football fan fiction.

### V3 content approach

Use a **curated game-content system**:

- hand-author the base scenario set
- choose concepts that fans can actually learn and recognize
- use AI only for bounded variation and explanation tone
- optimize for clarity, fairness, and replayability

### What fan content should focus on

- shell recognition
- leverage tells
- likely read progression
- where the ball should go
- blitz indicator
- route concept recognition
- down-and-distance logic

### What to avoid early

- hyper-granular coach-room jargon
- fake complexity for its own sake
- edge-case concepts only ex-coaches understand

The fan game should make people feel smart, not stupid.

## 8. Game Design

This is where the fan version either works or dies.

### The product needs a real game layer

Not superficial gamification.

A real game layer means:

- scoring
- rank movement
- visible wins and losses
- matchups
- progression
- repeatable challenge structure

### Recommended scoring system

Score users on:

- accuracy
- speed
- difficulty
- streak

### Recommended progression system

Use ranks, not educational tiers:

- Rookie
- Starter
- Veteran
- All-Pro
- Sunday Genius

These should be status ladders, not school labels.

### Public identity layer

Each user should have:

- Film Room score
- favorite team badge
- percentile ranking
- weekly record
- friend win-loss record

The app should create an identity worth defending.

---

## 9. Social and Viral Design

This version only works if the social loop is real.

### Strongest viral loop

Head-to-head challenges in existing football social channels:

- iMessage
- fantasy league group chats
- Discord

### Why this can work

Football fans already argue constantly.

Film Room gives them a structured way to compete on who sees the game best.

### Social product requirements

- one-tap challenge send
- easy result brag card
- visible "you beat X friends this week"
- small-group leaderboard
- weekly reset moments

### What not to rely on

- generic Instagram share cards
- broad public social graph
- passive follower systems

The real growth loop is group-chat-native competition.

---

## 10. Retention Design

For fan products, retention has to connect to an existing habit.

The existing habit is:

- checking football content daily
- watching NFL weekly
- talking football with friends constantly

Film Room should attach to those behaviors.

### Retention pillars

#### 1. Daily ritual

One challenge per day, fast and competitive.

#### 2. Weekly reset

New scoreboard every NFL week.

#### 3. Sunday utility

Use the app while watching games.

#### 4. Social pressure

Friends can challenge and compare performance.

#### 5. Personal progression

Users feel they are getting sharper over time.

Without Sunday relevance and social pressure, this becomes a novelty quiz app.

---

## 11. Monetization

The business model should match the fan product, not the original player-training product.

### Recommended model

#### Free

- daily challenge
- basic score profile
- limited friend challenges
- weekly leaderboard access

#### Premium subscription

`$8/month` or `$50/year`

Includes:

- unlimited friend challenges
- advanced game modes
- deeper explanation breakdowns
- team-specific challenge packs
- premium Sunday mode
- advanced stats and comparison tools
- historical performance tracking

### Why subscription can work here

Fans do pay for:

- status
- edge
- premium fantasy content
- better sports experiences

But they only pay if the product becomes part of their routine.

### What not to do

Do not lead with a one-time unlock.

That leaves too much value uncaptured if the product actually works.

---

## 12. Market Story

The right market story is not:

"There are 112M NFL fans."

That is lazy.

The right story is:

There is a large, high-engagement segment of NFL fans who already spend time and money on fantasy, betting content, football media, and social debate.

Film Room is targeting the most competitive and identity-driven slice of that behavior with a game product, not a generic education product.

### The venture case

If Film Room wins as a game-like social football companion, it can expand through:

1. deeper NFL fan modes
2. fantasy-adjacent tools
3. college football mode
4. sponsored leagues / branded competitions
5. premium creator or analyst content

That is a stronger consumer expansion path than trying to teach every fan formal football concepts.

---

## 13. Why This Could Be Venture-Backable

The fan version becomes venture-backable only if it proves four things:

### 1. It is a game, not a lesson

Users open it to play and compete, not to self-improve in theory.

### 2. It attaches to football rituals

Especially:

- daily football-content checking
- Sunday viewing
- fantasy chat behavior

### 3. It has social pull

Challenges and friend competition must drive real invites and repeat sessions.

### 4. It can monetize status and depth

Power users need to want more modes, more insight, and more competitive advantage.

Without those four things, it is just a smart quiz app.

## 15. Metrics That Matter

For the fan version, the metrics change.

The company should obsess over:

- daily challenge completion
- weekly active users during season
- invites sent per active user
- challenge acceptance rate
- sessions per user on Sundays
- 30-day retention in season
- premium conversion among power users

### Pre-seed proof targets

Fundraising gets meaningfully more credible if Film Room can show:

- 25%+ day-30 retention during NFL season
- strong Sunday usage concentration
- real friend-to-friend challenge sharing
- 10%+ of WAU sending challenges
- 5%+ premium conversion among retained users

These are not easy numbers.

That is the point.

The fan version only works if the social game loop is genuinely strong.

---

## 16. What Must Be True

These assumptions have to be tested quickly.

### Assumption 1

Fans enjoy reading and predicting football enough to return repeatedly.

### Assumption 2

Competition with friends is stronger than passive content consumption.

### Assumption 3

Sunday live behavior is enough to create a durable habit loop.

### Assumption 4

A meaningful segment will pay for deeper competition and insights.

If these are false, the fan-first strategy is not venture-backable.

---

## 17. Build Priorities

### Must build first

- polished scenario player
- clean scoring logic
- friend challenge flow
- leaderboard and profile identity
- weekly / Sunday retention loop

### Must not overbuild first

- endless AI generation
- complex role-based training
- giant glossary and education tab
- bloated social network features
- coach / team workflows

The first version should feel like a sharp, competitive football game.

---

## 18. Final Call

The fan-first version of Film Room can be more venture-backable than the player-training version, but only under one condition:

**it has to behave like a sports game with social competition, not like a learning app with some game wrappers.**

That means:

- faster sessions
- clearer score feedback
- stronger friend competition
- tighter Sunday relevance
- lighter teaching layer

If you get that right, the product has a real chance to become a differentiated football companion game.

If you get it wrong, it becomes a polished niche quiz app with weak retention.
